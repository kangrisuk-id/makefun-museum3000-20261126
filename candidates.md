# Museum of the Year 3000 — Candidates & Judging

Brief (from kit RULES.txt / competition.yaml): one single-line museum label that a
museum in the year 3000 puts beside an ordinary object from today. English,
original, 1–200 characters, written to `entry.txt`. Pairwise judge
(z-ai/glm-5.3-flash on OpenRouter), no ties.

Judge criteria, in the brief's own order (scored here 0–5 each, total /25):

- **C1 On-subject:** it IS a year-3000 museum label beside an ordinary object from today.
- **C2 First-read land:** the joke lands on a first read; the laugh is in the line itself.
- **C3 Misreading:** humour comes from the year 3000 misreading the object — true and absurd about what it was for.
- **C4 Label voice:** reads like a real museum label — short, straight-faced, confident, one line, no throat-clearing.
- **C5 Freshness:** object and angle are fresh, not the obvious first joke about the obvious first object.

## Knockout — top 8, head-to-head in the brief's criterion order

Seeds by total: 1 (#1, 24), 2 (#2, 24), 3 (#3, 23), 4 (#4, 23), 5 (#5, 22), 6 (#6, 22), 7 (#7, 22), 8 (#8, 22).

**Quarter-finals**
- #1 vs #8 (Gym Card): C1–C3 level-ish, C2 decides — the CAPTCHA reversal ("for one, unpaid") lands harder on first read than the gym tribute. **#1 wins.**
- #4 (Container) vs #5 (Router): C3 decides — "separate species" is the purer year-3000 misreading; the router's faith joke is warmer but softer. **#4 wins.**
- #2 (Silica) vs #7 (Receipt): C2 decides — DO NOT EAT plus the missing "incident" is an instant laugh; the scroll joke needs its second clause. **#2 wins.**
- #3 (Snooze) vs #6 (Cable): C2 decides — "lose the same argument with morning" is one clean beat; the cable needs the ritual explained. **#3 wins.**

**Semi-finals**
- #1 vs #4: C3 level (both true-and-absurd misreadings), C2 decides — proving-you-are-not-a-machine *to* a machine is the bigger first-read laugh, and every reader has lived it. **#1 wins.**
- #2 vs #3: C4 level (both exemplary label voice), C5 decides — the silica packet is the fresher plinth object; the snooze button is a well-worn target. **#2 wins.**

**Final — #1 vs #2 (both 24/25)**
- C1: level. C2: #1 — the laugh detonates in the final two words ("unpaid") after a
  perfectly straight build; #2's laugh is a smile that builds. C3: #1 — its absurdity is
  a documented truth of the age (humans training machines, free, to prove their own
  humanity), the deepest true-and-absurd misreading in the field. C4: #2 is marginally
  more label-like. C5: level. Per the brief, the first criterion that differs by much
  (C2, then C3) decides before C4 is reached. **Winner: #1.**
